Sources

The measles trial and the patient
  1. 1
    Remission of disseminated cancer after systemic oncolytic virotherapyMayo Clinic Proceedings, 2014 (Russell et al.). Supports the video: an engineered measles virus (MV-NIS) selectively destructive to myeloma cells, tracked by radioiodine imaging; two measles-seronegative patients with drug-resistant myeloma; one durable complete remission; side effects resolved within the first week.
  2. 2
    Virotherapy against multiple myeloma (news release)Mayo Clinic News Network, May 2014. Supports the video: a proof-of-principle trial in two patients with limited prior measles exposure; the second patient responded less fully; a larger trial was planned; the researchers disclosed a financial interest.
  3. 3
    Modified measles helped her beat cancer. Ten years later, she returned to Rochester to celebratePost Bulletin (local news), 2023. Supports the video: diagnosis in 2004, two stem cell transplants and chemotherapy, the June 5, 2013 thirty-minute infusion described as enough virus to vaccinate 10 million people, the forehead tumor gone within about 36 hours, a brief course of radiation, cancer-free ten years later. The woman is not named in the video.
History and how virus therapy works
  1. 1
    History of oncolytic viruses: genesis to genetic engineeringMolecular Therapy, 2007 (Kelly and Russell). Supports the video: early reports of tumors shrinking during virus infections, deliberate infections with body fluids from humans or animals, frequent failure because of the immune system or harm to the patient, researchers abandoning the field, and the revival through genetic engineering.
  2. 2
    Oncolytic viruses: a new class of immunotherapy drugsNature Reviews Drug Discovery, 2015 (Kaufman et al.). Supports the video: viruses replicate in and kill tumor cells, interact with tumor cells' antiviral defenses, and trigger anti-tumor immunity; many native and engineered viruses are in development.
  3. 3
    Oncolytic virotherapyNature Biotechnology, 2012 (Russell, Peng and Bell). Supports the video: a critical level of virus in the blood is needed for delivery to tumors; key challenges include balancing the immune system (briefly suppress, then unleash) and manufacturing enough virus.
The first approved virus therapy
  1. 1
    Talimogene Laherparepvec Improves Durable Response Rate in Patients With Advanced MelanomaJournal of Clinical Oncology, 2015 (Andtbacka et al.). Supports scene 32: durable response rate 16.3% with the engineered herpes virus versus 2.1% with the comparison treatment.
  2. 2
    FDA approves IMLYGIC as first oncolytic viral therapy in the USAmgen press release (the manufacturer), 27 October 2015. Supports the video: approval for local treatment of melanoma lesions; it has not been shown to improve overall survival or to affect metastases in the organs.
Added in this version: why measles
  1. 1
    Oncolytic measles virus targets high CD46 expression on multiple myeloma cellsExperimental Hematology, 2006 (Ong et al.). Supports scenes 13-14: myeloma cells carried about 49,000 CD46 receptors per cell vs about 7,300 on surrounding non-plasma cells; infected myeloma cells fused into giant cells and died, while normal bone marrow cells were spared.
  2. 2
    Measles to the Rescue: A Review of Oncolytic Measles VirusViruses, 2016 (Aref et al.). Supports scene 16A: the vaccine strain of measles uses the CD46 receptor, which many tumour cells overexpress.

A note on wording

The hospital morning, the cell catching a cold, the inside of a tumor and the future clinic are dramatizations marked with "picture". The patient is drawn as a simple stickman, not as the real person, and is not named. This was a first test in two people: one reached complete remission, the other responded less fully, and one success does not show how often the treatment works. "Many cancer cells have lost parts of this defense" is a simplification that does not apply to every cancer. The ten-year update comes from a local news report, not a medical paper. Virus therapy is not a cure for cancer. This video is for information only and is not medical advice; if you or someone you love has cancer, talk to the medical team before making any treatment decision.
